# Lab Instructions

In this lab, you will analyze two real world systems failures that have been well documented: the Rogers 2022 outage and the AWS 2025 outage.

Both cases are similar, and are characteristic of the type of cascading failures that we often see in operational systems.

This is a group exercise.

There is no "right answer" for this lab. Rather, it is an exercise in critical analysis from an operational perspective.

Each group should analyze both of the case students and provide a report and analysis on the following.

1. Track how the outage started, how it turned into a cascading failure and how it propagated and spread. Identify the blast radius of each failure.
2. Identify the "knee" of the failures in terms of when they reached a tipping point. Explain the roles that resource saturation and the "thundering herd" might have played. 
3. Identify and describe the dependencies that enabled the failures to progress. Identify how these dependencies enabled cascade of failures and increased the outage blast radius. Draw a diagram if you need to in order to visualize what happened.
4. Often, the triggering event is some change that takes place in the system. For each case, identify the change or changes that were the catalytic events that led to the outage. 


In the following units, we will return to these two case studies and look at how we would do risk management and resilience planning for these systems.

For your group's presentation, stick to an analysis that is grounded in only the facts presented. Do not offer recommendations for corrective action, you will do that later. Just focus on developing a critical analysis of the events as they happened.
