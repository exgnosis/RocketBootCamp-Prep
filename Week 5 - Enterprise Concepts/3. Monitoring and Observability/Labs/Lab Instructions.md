# Lab Instructions

For the next two modules, the labs will all deal with a system outage at a fictitious Internet service company where there has been a catastrophic failure. The failure has been fully documented for you, including the analysis done by the operations team.

The goal of the next two labs is to turn that analysis in to a preliminary plan for managing the risks that led to the outage, and developing a plan for recovery when an outage occurs.

The Rogers and AWS case studies from the previous section were used as a basis cor this invented case to make it as realistic as possible, so you can refer to those for ideas. 

## Set up

The company that suffered the outage does not have the internal resources to perform the analysis and planning internally 

Your group has been brought in as external consultants to 
- Review the company’s risk management posture 
- Make recommendations for a risk strategy 
- Analyze and make recommendations on risk controls and operating procedures
- Recommend changes in the current corporate approach to managing future risk
  
## Scenario

You will start with the initial internal assessment of the failure which is in the file `Incident Report.pdf`
- Not all the data is relevant, you have to sift through it to find what you need
    - One of the challenges we often face is to separate the usefuld data with respect to a problem from all the "noise" or irrelevant data
- The data is incomplete, you have to make reasonable assumptions to fill in the gaps.
- The data is messy, it’s not totally accurate or up to date and may require clarification. Identify what other data you need that is not provided and how the lack of it affects your assessment.

You will be provided with

- A corporate overview for background. This includes a detailed description of the IT architecture and business organization
- The postmortem incident report from the failure investigation
- Supporting documentation

## Deliverables

For this lab, address the following issues

1. What sort of controls, preventative, detective and correcting, were in place prior to the incident?
2. Why did the controls fail to prevent or detect the failure until after the fact?
3. What sort of recovery processes were in place to restore service after a failure?
4. What sort of internal processes, SOPs and runbooks were available to allow fast response to an outage?
5. Based on the previous four points, recommend changes to existing controls and procedures that would either prevent failures for occurring in the future, identify failures early enough to allow for a timely response, and how to protect the IT assets to allow for a quick and reliable recovery of operations.
